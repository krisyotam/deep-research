# **A Comprehensive Directory of Blogs Focused on Western Classics, Classical Literature, and Formal Poetry**

**1. Introduction: Defining the Digital Landscape of Western Classics, Classical Literature, and Formal Poetry Blogs**

This report aims to provide a comprehensive directory of blogs centered on the Western Great Books tradition, classical literature, and formal poetry. The research methodology involved a deep web search utilizing targeted queries focused on identifying relevant blogs hosted on specific platforms, including Blogger (blogspot.com), WordPress (wordpress.com), self-hosted static sites, and GitHub-hosted blogs. The scope of this investigation encompasses blogs written by or for various audience groups, such as Great Books readers, students of Western literature, classical educators, poets working in formal verse, literary critics and essayists, scholars of Greek, Latin, and the European canon, and philosophy and humanities students. The proliferation of digital platforms has significantly altered the landscape of literary discourse, with blogs emerging as accessible and dynamic spaces for readers, students, and scholars to engage with these enduring subjects.

To establish a clear framework for this report, it is essential to define the key terms that underpin its focus. The **Western Great Books Tradition** generally refers to a curated list of influential works from Western culture, often considered foundational to its intellectual and artistic heritage. This tradition typically includes texts spanning from ancient Greece to the modern era, featuring authors such as Homer, Plato, Dante Alighieri, and William Shakespeare. The underlying premise of this tradition is often framed as a "Great Conversation," an ongoing dialogue of ideas and themes that resonates across centuries. This suggests that blogs dedicated to this tradition may explore the historical trajectory of these core ideas and the ways in which different authors and texts engage with one another over time. Furthermore, the very definition and composition of the Western Canon are subjects of ongoing discussion and re-evaluation. Blogs in this area might therefore reflect these debates, potentially offering diverse perspectives on which works are included and why, and perhaps challenging traditional boundaries to incorporate a wider range of voices and perspectives.

**Classical Literature** is a broader category that typically encompasses the literature of ancient Greece and Rome, as well as the subsequent European literary tradition that drew heavily upon these foundations, often extending up to the Renaissance period. Key genres within classical literature include epic poetry (such as Homer's *Iliad* and Virgil's *Aeneid*), tragic and comic drama (by playwrights like Sophocles and Aristophanes), philosophical dialogues (most notably those of Plato), and historical accounts (from writers such as Thucydides). The enduring appeal and relevance of classical literature are evident in ongoing contemporary readings and analyses of these works, such as the continued engagement with Homer's *Iliad*. This suggests that blogs focusing on classical literature might explore these texts through modern critical lenses, drawing connections between ancient themes and contemporary concerns. Moreover, the accessibility of these texts has been greatly enhanced by the availability of online resources and a multitude of translations. Blogs in this category may serve as valuable guides to these resources, offering recommendations and comparative analyses of different translations to aid readers in their exploration.

**Formal Poetry** is characterized by its adherence to specific structural conventions, including prescribed meters (such as iambic pentameter) 11, established rhyme schemes, and traditional poetic forms like the sonnet, villanelle, sestina, and pantoum. This contrasts with free verse, which typically does not follow such strict patterns. A notable aspect of contemporary poetic discourse is the ongoing discussion surrounding the merits and relevance of formal poetry in relation to free verse. Blogs dedicated to formal poetry might engage with this debate, offering arguments for the continued value and artistic potential of traditional forms, while also providing analyses of their techniques and effects. Despite the prevalence of free verse in modern poetry, formal poetry continues to be practiced and appreciated by many contemporary poets. Blogs in this area may serve as platforms for showcasing new formal verse and providing resources for poets interested in honing their craft within these established structures.

In the contemporary literary landscape, blogs have emerged as significant platforms for literary discourse. They offer accessibility for both writers and readers, breaking down traditional barriers to publication and critical engagement. These digital spaces foster communities of like-minded individuals, allowing for the exchange of ideas, the sharing of resources, and the development of new perspectives on established literary traditions. For those interested in the Western Great Books, classical literature, and formal poetry, blogs provide a rich and diverse ecosystem of information, analysis, and personal engagement.
